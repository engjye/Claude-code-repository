Chromavii
—Prologue

a story told in seven colors

Chroma

Before the world had a name,
it had a color.

begin
I.
Crimson — the first cry

The beginning was red.

It was not anger. It was not love. It was simply the colour of becoming — the bright, startled red of a heart learning that it has a pulse, of a sky split open by its first dawn.

Every story begins as a wound that learns to sing.

And then, slowly, the red began to remember.

II.
Ochre — the colour of memory

It walked, golden, into the afternoon.

Ochre is the dust on old photographs, the patient light of empty rooms, the breath of pages held too long. It is the colour of things we keep without knowing why.

Some hues do not shout — they wait, like grandmothers, like fields, like wheat.

III.
Cerulean — the colour of longing

Then came the blue.

Blue is the long pause between two heartbeats. It is the colour of distances we choose, of the ocean we paint inside ourselves, of a window left open all night.

To miss something is to know the precise weight of its absence — and to call that weight, gently, blue.

And the world, having grieved, turned green.

IV.
Verdigris — the colour of return

Something inside us learned to grow again.

Verdigris is the patience of copper, the soft revolt of moss, the slow rumour of a leaf returning to a branch we thought was finished. It is the colour of forgiveness.

Nothing ever truly ends; it only changes pigment.

V.
Gold — the colour of arrival

And the light, at last, said yes.

Gold is the hour a city stops to look at itself. It is the warm hush of late summer, the patient gleam of something well-loved, well-used, well-kept.

To be golden is not to shine — it is to warm.

VI.
Lilac — the colour of falling asleep

Then dusk arrived, unhurried.

Lilac is the colour of dreams that don't bother to make sense. It is the slow lavender of an evening that has nowhere to be, of a child being carried in from the car, half-asleep, into the rest of their life.

Goodbyes, when they're kind, are always a little purple.

vii — every colour, at once

And the page was whole again.

White is not the absence of colour.
It is every colour, holding hands.
